How Materials, Collections, and Work Types fit together

A Material is one specific product (a SKU, model, or color) with its own cost. Every material belongs to exactly one Work Type — that's what makes it show up in that work type's picker on a quote. A "Jandy 1.65HP" pump is a material; its Work Type is Pump Installation.

A Collection is the brand or manufacturer (Jandy, Pentair, Keystone Tile). It's optional, but once a work type has more than a handful of materials, grouping them under a Collection narrows the picker on the quote screen instead of dumping everything into one long list. Category / Sub Category do the same job at a finer level within a work type or collection.

Setting up a new work type that needs both labor and a material (e.g. a new piece of equipment): add the sub's install rate in Sub Rates — that's the labor. Add the product(s) here, scoped to that Work Type — that's the material. A line item automatically becomes a labor + material split the moment both are picked on a quote; there's nothing else to configure.

Cost Structure on a work type (in Work Types) doesn't change how anything prices — it's just a label, purely cosmetic.
